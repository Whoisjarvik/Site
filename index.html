Hello World

2026-02-23

This is the first post on my blog. I wanted to start writing about the things I find interesting, the projects I'm working on, and the ideas that keep me up at night. Writing has always been a way for me to process my thoughts and make sense of the world around me.

There's something about putting words on a page that forces clarity. When an idea lives only in your head, it can feel complete and polished. But the moment you try to write it down, you realize how many gaps there are, how many assumptions you've been making, and how much more thinking you need to do.

I've been spending a lot of time recently thinking about how we build things on the internet. The tools we use shape the things we create in ways we don't always appreciate. A blog built with a static site generator feels different from one built on WordPress, which feels different from one on Substack. Each platform nudges you toward certain kinds of expression and away from others.

For this site, I wanted something as simple as possible. A single HTML file. No build step, no dependencies, no framework. Just words on a dark screen. There's a certain honesty to that approach. What you see is literally all there is.

I think we've overcomplicated web development. Not everything needs to be a React app. Not everything needs a database. Sometimes you just want to write something and put it on the internet, and a plain HTML file is the most direct way to do that.

Of course, simplicity has trade-offs. There's no comment system, no analytics, no newsletter signup. But those are features that serve the platform more than they serve the reader. If someone wants to respond to something I've written, they can reach me on Twitter or send me an email. That feels more human anyway.

I don't know how often I'll write here. Maybe weekly, maybe monthly, maybe whenever the mood strikes. I'm not going to set a schedule because I don't want writing to feel like an obligation. The best writing comes from genuine curiosity and excitement, not from a content calendar.

What I do know is that I want to write about things that matter to me. Technology, design, the creative process, the occasional philosophical tangent. If even one person finds something useful or interesting here, that's enough.

So here we are. Hello world. Let's see where this goes.

Project One

This project started as a weekend experiment and turned into something much bigger. The initial idea was simple: build a tool that solves a specific problem I kept running into. But as I dug deeper, I realized the problem was more nuanced than I thought.

The first version was rough. I built it in a single afternoon, cutting every corner I could. But it worked, and that was enough to validate the idea. Sometimes you need to see something working, even badly, to understand whether it's worth pursuing.

Over the next few weeks, I rebuilt it from scratch. This time I paid attention to the details that matter: performance, edge cases, the small interactions that make software feel good to use. The architecture evolved as my understanding of the problem deepened.

One of the biggest lessons from this project was about scope. It's tempting to keep adding features, to keep polishing, to keep making things "better." But at some point you have to ship. Perfect is the enemy of done, and done is what actually helps people.

The technical stack was deliberately simple. I wanted to minimize dependencies and keep the codebase small enough that I could hold the whole thing in my head. That constraint forced me to make better design decisions and avoid unnecessary complexity.

Looking back, I'm proud of what I built. Not because it's the most impressive piece of engineering, but because it solves a real problem in a clean, straightforward way. That's the kind of software I want to make.
